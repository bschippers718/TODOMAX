Direction 01

Signal.

Your to‑do list as subway signage. Black ink, white enamel, one yellow route and nine line colors doing all the work. Nothing soft, nothing decorative — every mark means something.

✓ 4 stops today · Uptown via Dentist

Why this direction

Borrow what made the viral ones legible at a glance.

Signal takes the Errand Run concept literally — the map, the route, the stops — and renders it in the most legible visual system ever shipped to millions of people a day: transit wayfinding.

Wordle: the result is the ad

Wordle grew on a green/yellow/grey grid that told the whole story without spoiling anything. Signal gives ToDOMax its own grid: the Daily Route strip — one square per task, line‑colored by outcome. It's screenshot‑native and readable in a feed at thumbnail size.

thehardcopy.co · smithsonianmag.com

Clear: color = priority, no buttons

Clear (2012) sold 350k copies in two weeks on a heat‑map list and pure gestures. Signal keeps our swipe‑to‑strike and adds line‑color priority: the top stop is Express blue, the rest are Local. One glance tells you what matters.

techcrunch.com · technologyreview.com

Flighty: one signal color

Flighty's palette is almost achromatic with a single vivid blue for actions and amber for conversion. Signal reserves Route Yellow for the one thing you can do next, and Express Blue for the one thing you can buy.

refero.design · mobbin.com

Neo‑brutalist UI (2–4px black borders, hard offset shadows, industrial yellows/oranges/greens) is the most active indie‑app territory of 2026 — but it only works when the palette is tightly constrained to 3–5 colors with a single dominant background. Signal's discipline is: paper, ink, route, plus line colors used strictly as semantics.

Viral artifact

The Daily Route card.

Wordle's lesson: the thing people share should be the synopsis of their day, not a screenshot of the app. One square per task, line‑colored by outcome, no task text — so it's safe to post and legible at thumbnail size.

How it works

  • Trigger: after the last task of the day is struck, the celebration ends on the card with a single "Share route" button. Not a popup — it's the end of the movie.
  • Scarcity: one card per day, generated at the moment you clear the board. Can't be re‑rolled.
  • Common knowledge: only people who use ToDOMax can read the colors. That's the point — it's a signal to the in‑group, same as Wordle's grid.
  • Streak is on the card, because streaks are about not losing (Mobbin's study of 859 streak designs). A 27 next to the squares is the stake.
  • Attribution: a quiet URL in the footer. Loom's 3‑second outro, Calendly's footer — small, permanent, never a watermark.

Motion & feel

Mechanical, not bouncy.

PressSign drops onto its shadow: translate(3,3), shadow → 0. 80 ms, no easing curve to speak of.
StrikeKeep the pen scribble as‑is. Stop Red, 5px, zero radius ends — a grease pencil, not a marker.
CollapseHeight collapses with the overdamped spring already shipped; the hard shadow collapses with it.
CelebrationsKit animations run unchanged. Their banners already use these colors (bannerRed, bannerYellow). Add a 2px ink border to the banner.
HapticsUnchanged: light ticks per stroke, Success on land, selection on arm.
Reduce MotionHard shadows don't move; only the drop. Already compatible.

Rules

Do / Don't

Do
  • Use Route Yellow for exactly one element per screen.
  • Give every line color a meaning before it appears.
  • Keep borders 2.5px, radius ≤ 6px, shadows hard and offset.
  • Let text do the work — Archivo 900 at 34px+ is the hero image.
  • Use Press Start 2P at 6–8px only, uppercase, inside a bordered tag.
Don't
  • No gradients, no blur, no translucency. Signage is opaque.
  • No map image behind the list — the route is the strip, not wallpaper.
  • No soft shadows or rounded‑rect "cards"; a sign is a sign.
  • Don't tint the dark mode grey; it's black.
  • Never put task text on the share card.
