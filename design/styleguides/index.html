Three ways ToDOMax could look.

You said you weren't sold on the current colors and style. These are three deliberately different directions — each built on the same product (pixel celebrations, packs, collection, streak) but with its own palette, type, tone, and, importantly, its own shareable artifact. The research below is why that last part matters more than the colors.

Each guide is a single HTML page: palette with contrast checks, type specimens, every component we have today redrawn, light + dark home screens, the viral artifact, motion notes, do/don'ts, and a mapping table to lib/theme.ts so any direction can be adopted without a rewrite.

Research

Why apps go viral (and what it means for a to‑do app).

Short version: nobody shares an app because of its colors. They share an artifact the app made about them, and they come back because leaving would lose something. Style matters because it makes the artifact recognisable at thumbnail size.

1. A native, text‑safe artifact

Wordle's grid went viral because it was spoiler‑free, instantly recognisable, and pastable into any chat. BeReal's two‑camera frame and Duolingo's streak screenshots work the same way: the output is the ad.

AppliedEach direction ships a share card that contains no task text (privacy), carries the streak, and is unmistakably ToDOMax at 200px wide.
Wordle's "elegant" share grid analysis; Duolingo growth retros

2. Loss aversion beats reward

Mobbin's study of 859 streak implementations: streaks work through fear of losing, not joy of gaining. Duolingo's Streak Freeze lifted retention; friend streaks lifted commitment 22%. Finch shows a repair hammer instead of a zero.

AppliedEvery direction has a visible streak and a forgiveness mechanic (Snow Day / Continue? / scaffolding) so a miss is a near‑loss, not an exit.
mobbin.com streak study; blog.duolingo.com

3. Personality is the moat

Clear (2012) sold 350k copies in a week with no buttons and a heat‑map list — people shared videos of a to‑do app. Balatro's pixel identity made it recognisable in a sea of card games. Generic doesn't travel.

AppliedEach direction has one unmissable signature: Signal's hard shadows, Arcade's glow + scanlines, Soft Play's growing block.
Clear launch coverage; Balatro design write‑ups

4. One signal color

Flighty (Apple Design Award) is achromatic with exactly one blue and one yellow. When a product has one color, that color becomes a logo. Our current palette has five competing accents.

AppliedEach direction names a single primary action color (Route Yellow / Coin / Coral) and demotes the rest to semantic roles.
Flighty design notes; UXPin 2026 color psychology

5. Fast to first value, tiny commitment

Wordle is one puzzle a day. Duolingo's "Commit to my goal" button copy added 10k daily returns. The ask has to be small and the payoff immediate.

Applied"Add one thing" as the add‑bar prompt across all three; the 3‑second celebration is already the fastest payoff in the category.
mobbin.com; Duolingo A/B retros

6. Quiet attribution

Loom, Calendly, and Linear grew through the footer of things their users made. Attribution works when it's small and classy, not when it's a watermark.

AppliedShare cards carry todomax.app in the footer at ≤ 12px, never a logo lockup over the content.
PLG case studies (Loom, Calendly, Linear)

Recommendation

Lead with Arcade After Dark's identity, borrow Signal's discipline.

The 8‑bit celebrations are the only thing in this category nobody else has, and Arcade is the only direction where the UI and the celebrations speak the same language — the app would look like its best feature. Signal's rules (one action colour, hard geometry, text‑safe share grid) are the ones that keep it from becoming a novelty. Soft Play's growing block is the strongest retention idea of the three and is portable: it works as a pixel skyline in Arcade just as well.

Honest counter‑case: if the business goal is the broadest possible audience (families, non‑gamers), Soft Play is the safer bet and the block/postcard loop is the best growth engine. Arcade is a sharper wedge; Soft Play is a wider net.

  1. Read all three in order; note gut reactions before reading the comparison.
  2. Decide default mode: dark‑first (Arcade) or light‑first (Signal / Soft Play). This single choice removes one direction.
  3. Pick the artifact you'd personally post. That's the one worth building first; the palette follows it.
Who is the person we picture?Commuter with a dry sense of humour (Signal), gamer who tracks stats (Arcade), or someone who's tried five habit apps (Soft Play)?
Is dark‑first acceptable?Arcade's day variant works but is clearly the B‑side. Most daytime screenshots would be dark.
Social or solo?High Scores and friend streets both need friends. Daily Route and the postcard work solo. How soon do we want accounts?
How much of the kit is sacred?Signal and Soft Play recolour nothing in the kit. Arcade would want its cabinet bezel around the scenes.
Name and icon.None of the three were designed around the current icon. Once a direction is picked, the icon should be re‑cut in it.
Blend?A fourth option is Signal's layout + Arcade's palette in dark, Soft Play's block as the hero. Say the word and I'll draft it.
