Direction 03

Soft Play.

Pixel art, but the kind you want to hug. Butter backgrounds, rounded everything, candy accents — and a little pixel block that builds itself up as you finish things. The mass‑market direction: friendly enough for your mum, playful enough to screenshot.

Why this direction

The apps that stuck gave you something to take care of.

Streaks are about not losing. Finch and Duolingo both learned that a number is easy to abandon, but a thing you've been raising isn't. Soft Play gives ToDOMax a thing: your block — a row of pixel buildings that grows one storey per task.

Finch: progress you can see grow

"Instead of tracking your habits, you're raising something." Finch's bird grows when you show up and doesn't when you don't — and a missed day shows a repair hammer, not a blank. Soft Play's block does the same: a storey is added per task, and a quiet day shows scaffolding you can still finish.

mobbin.com — 859 streak designs

Duolingo: "Commit to my goal"

Changing one button from Continue to Commit to my goal brought 10,000 more people back daily. Soft Play's copy is first‑person and warm: "Add one thing", "Done for today", "Keep my street lit". The product talks like a friend, not a system.

blog.duolingo.com · mobbin.com

Headspace: calm sells

Headspace's muted oranges and soft blues deliberately avoid sharp contrast. Productivity apps skew anxious; the one that doesn't is the one people recommend. Soft Play keeps saturation high but tone warm — candy, not neon.

uxpin.com — app color psychology (2026)

Risk to name honestly: this is the most crowded aesthetic (Finch, Habitica, Forest, Streaks). The differentiator has to be the pixel block + the kit's cinematic celebrations — nobody in this space has a 3‑second pixel movie when you finish a task.

Every candy has a "deep" partner used as a 4–8px bottom edge on buttons and badges — the chunky, pressable look (Duolingo's buttons work this way). The pastel is the face; the deep is the side.

Typography

Round and heavy, with a pixel wink.

Nunito at 800–900 everywhere. One family keeps it simple and friendly; the weight keeps it from going twee. Silkscreen — a cleaner, rounder bitmap than Press Start — only for 7–9px labels and the pixel block's captions.

Viral artifact

The postcard.

At the end of each week (or month) your block becomes a postcard: the skyline you built, the streak, no task text. It's the Finch bird photo, the Duolingo year‑in‑review, the thing people post because it's theirs.

Ben's block · Week 4023 things finished · 7 buildings · 27‑day streak 🔥
Greetings from ToDOMaxtodomax.app

How it works

  • Generated, not screenshotted. The postcard is rendered from your week's data — tall buildings on big days, scaffolding on quiet ones. Every one is different.
  • Identity: "Ben's block." The research is blunt — people share things that are about them. The skyline is a portrait.
  • Repair, don't punish. A missed day is a dashed building you can still complete tomorrow (Finch's hammer). The streak never resets to zero mid‑week; it pauses.
  • Friend streets: add a friend and your blocks sit side by side — the 22% friend‑streak lift, without a leaderboard.
  • Packs show up on the skyline. Earn a Game Day celebration and a stadium appears; Garage adds a gas station. The store becomes world‑building.

Motion & feel

Squishy, warm, quick.

PressButtons depress 6px onto their deep edge and spring back (damping 14, stiffness 320). Scale stays 1.
StrikeExisting scribble in Coral, slightly thicker (5px, round caps). The checkbox fills Mint and pops to 1.15 on land.
Block growsAfter the celebration, today's building gains a storey with a 180 ms overshoot spring. This is the second payoff — tiny, every time.
CelebrationsKit scenes unchanged; overlay backdrop is Butter at 60% instead of ink, so the movie feels like a daydream rather than a cut to black.
SoundKeep the kit sounds; consider a soft "plink" for the storey landing.
Reduce MotionButtons don't depress; the block updates without spring. Already supported by the current hook.

Rules

Do / Don't

Do
  • Pair every candy with its deep edge; the pastel alone looks flat.
  • Keep radius ≥ 18px on cards, 999 on buttons and inputs.
  • Write copy in first person and present tense — "Add one thing", "Done for today".
  • Let the block be the only illustration on the home screen.
  • Use Silkscreen for ≤ 9px labels only.
Don't
  • No gradients on controls — only on the hero wash and postcard sky.
  • No pure black, no grey text below 4.5 : 1 for body copy.
  • Don't add a mascot character. The block is the companion; a bird would make it Finch.
  • Don't reset the streak to zero mid‑week. Pause it.
  • No more than two candies per component.
