Why this direction

The celebrations are already pixel art. Stop fighting them.

Today the kit animations (bannerRed, typeGreen, LCD watch) play on top of a cream paper UI — two visual systems sharing a screen. Arcade makes the UI and the celebrations one world, so a completed task feels like clearing a level, not opening a popup.

Balatro: personality beats polish

A poker roguelike with CRT shader and hand‑drawn pixel jokers became 2024–25's word‑of‑mouth hit. Not because it was technically impressive — because it was unmistakable in a 2‑second clip. Arcade gives ToDOMax that: nobody else's to‑do list glows.

Duolingo: a streak you defend

Mobbin's study of 859 streak designs: the sting of losing a streak is about who you are, not the number. Arcade reframes the streak as a HI SCORE you're defending — the oldest loss‑aversion loop in gaming, and one every player already understands.

Flighty: the control‑room drop

Flighty's page goes from white departure board to a "deep midnight control room with glowing screens". Its dark mode is the premium mode. Arcade is dark‑first for the same reason; the "day" variant is the fallback, not the default.

refero.design — Flighty design system

Caution from the research: dark‑first UIs need desaturated accents (fully saturated colors vibrate on dark) and strict contrast checks. Every phosphor color below has been pulled back from pure neon and tested against the cabinet surfaces.

Viral artifact

The high score table.

Every arcade ends on the same screen, and everyone knows how to read it. A weekly table: your streak and tasks cleared against your friends' initials, plus the cartridges you've earned. BeReal proved social stakes with a tiny mechanic; this is ours.

How it works

  • Initials, not names. Three letters is the most private leaderboard ever invented — and it's fun to pick them.
  • Streak = score. It's defended, not accumulated. Losing your place on the table is the loss‑aversion loop, with the friend layer Duolingo found lifts daily completion 22%.
  • Cartridge shelf shows earned celebrations as colored carts — the Collection board, compressed into one line.
  • Weekly reset. A new table every Monday keeps newcomers in the game (BeReal's daily window, stretched to a week).
  • Solo mode: with no friends added, the table shows your past four weeks instead — you versus you.

Snappy, lit, a little electric.

Scanlines3.5% overlay on screen backgrounds only. Never on text containers. Off under Reduce Transparency.

Rules

Do / Don't

Do
  • Glow only Coin, Power and Laser — and only at 20–40% alpha.
  • Keep bezels 1px; depth comes from gradient + glow, not borders.
  • Use Press Start 2P for marquees ≥ 14px or tags ≤ 7px. Nothing in between.
  • Tabular, zero‑padded numbers everywhere a score appears.
  • Ship dark as default; treat day as the accessibility fallback.
Don't
  • No pure black (#000) and no pure neon (#0F0, #F0F). Both read cheap.
  • No pixel font in body copy or task text. Ever.
  • No more than one glowing element in a card.
  • Don't add a CRT curvature or chromatic aberration — scanlines are the limit.
  • Don't show friends' real names on the table.
